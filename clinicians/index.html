Where Orien fits

Orien is a bridge, not a replacement. A full AAC evaluation and a dedicated eye gaze device are still the right path for many people.

A good fit

  • While waiting on an AAC evaluation, insurance approval or device delivery.
  • As a backup or second device: at the bedside, in the car, or when the main device is charging or in for repair.
  • For people who don't qualify for a funded device, or whose family can't wait.
  • Early on, to get comfortable with eye gaze before speech or hand use declines.

Look elsewhere when

  • The person needs a large vocabulary, fast typing or full device control. A dedicated eye gaze device or iOS Eye Tracking is a better match.
  • Looks up, down, left and right aren't reliable.
  • They don't have an iPhone with Face ID. iPhone SE won't work, and iPad isn't supported yet.
  • They need a way to call for help in an emergency. Orien isn't an emergency system.

Feature matching at a glance

What Orien does today, version 1.0, so you can match it to your patient.

Access method
Eye gaze through the iPhone's Face ID (TrueDepth) camera, using Apple's face tracking. Orien follows the eyes, not the head. Touch works at any time.
Calibration
None. Open the app and start.
Targets
Four large targets per screen, at the top, bottom, left and right edges. Look down to go back.
Selection
Dwell until the bar fills, or a deliberate blink with both eyes (0.15 to 0.6 seconds). Both are always on in this version.
Speed
Standard, Fast, Expert or Instant. On Standard, opening a menu takes a 3 second dwell and speaking a phrase takes about 6.5 seconds.
Spelling
An eye controlled keyboard with a gaze dot and a 1 second dwell per key. Gaze dot sensitivity adjusts separately for horizontal and vertical.
Vocabulary
Urgent phrases come first: "Help me now," "I'm in pain," "Call someone" and "Stop." Then needs (food, drink, bathroom, comfort, position, medical), feelings with pain location (head, chest, stomach, back, legs), requests (TV, lights, music), greetings, yes, no and maybe, questions, and personal information.
People
Speaks a contact's name with a phrase, like "Mom, can you come here?" Prepares texts in Apple Messages; sending needs a tap. No phone calls.
Speech output
On the device, in any installed English iOS voice. Personal Voice isn't supported in this version.
When the face is lost
Selection pauses, and Orien shows and says "Face not detected." Touch still works.
Training
A guided tour on first launch, which you can replay from Settings.
Devices
iPhone XR, XS or newer with Face ID, on iOS 17 or later. Not iPhone SE. iPad isn't supported yet.
Cost and privacy
Free. No account, ads, analytics or servers. Nothing leaves the phone.
Classification
A communication aid. Not a medical device, and not an emergency service.

Try it in one visit

About 15 minutes, on the family's own iPhone.

  1. Install OrienSearch "Orien Speak with Eyes" in the App Store, or scan the code on the handout. It's free, with no account.

  2. Position the phoneMount it at eye level, in front of the face, about as far away as for a FaceTime call. Put light on the face, with no bright window behind.

  3. Allow the cameraContacts and Photos are optional.

  4. Run the guided tourWhen OK appears, look at it or tap it, then enter a name. You can tap the keys. Orien walks through each area in about two minutes.

  5. Try the urgent phrasesLook up to open E‑Talk and choose "I'm in pain." Watch for misfires and how long each selection takes.

  6. Set the speedSettings is on the third page, on the left. Start on Standard and move to Fast as accuracy holds.

  7. Send the handout homeWrite your name and number at the bottom so the family knows who to call.

Questions clinicians ask

Is Orien a speech generating device that insurance pays for?

No. Orien is a free app, so there's nothing to bill and no paperwork. It can run alongside the process of getting a funded speech generating device, and serve as a backup once that device arrives.

Does Orien need calibration?

No. Orien reads the direction of each eye from the Face ID camera and maps it to four large targets, so there's no calibration step. The gaze dot used on the keyboard has separate horizontal and vertical sensitivity settings.

Does Orien collect patient data?

No. Orien has no accounts, analytics or servers, and the app contains no networking code. Camera images, face data and messages stay on the phone. There is no patient information for us to receive.

What if my patient's eye movement is limited?

Orien needs reliable looks up, down, left and right. If that isn't possible, a dedicated eye gaze device or switch access is the better fit today. We're exploring options for reduced eye movement, and clinician input shapes them.

Can my patient text or call someone with Orien?

Orien can speak a message to someone by name, like "Mom, can you come here?", and prepare a text in Apple Messages. iOS requires a tap to send the text. Orien doesn't place phone calls.

Is there a cost to share the family handout?

No. Print it, copy it and hand it out as often as you like. It's one page and prints well in black and white.
